Apartment Moving Day Checklist for Dubai Residents

Moving day gets messy when small jobs are left until the movers arrive. A few hours can disappear while people look for keys, finish packing, clear hallways, or work out how furniture will leave the building.

An apartment move becomes easier when the important details are handled before the truck arrives. The following checklist focuses on the parts that are easy to overlook.

Finish Packing Before the Crew Arrives

Moving day should not be the main packing day.

Clothes, books, kitchen items, documents and smaller belongings should already be packed and labeled. Fragile kitchenware needs a little more attention, so how to pack fragile items is worth reviewing before boxing up glassware, plates and other delicate belongings.

That personal bag can include:

  • Phone and charger
  • Wallet and keys
  • Important documents
  • Basic toiletries
  • Medication
  • Water
  • A change of clothes
  • Snacks

This keeps essential items separate from the boxes going into the truck.

Keep Important Items With You

Some belongings should not go into the moving truck. Important documents, jewelry, cash, passports and other valuable personal items are better kept with you.

The same applies to anything you may need immediately after reaching the new apartment.

Separating these items early prevents them from being mixed with household boxes.

Check the Apartment Before Loading

Take a quick walk through every room before the first item leaves.

Check cupboards, drawers, shelves, balconies and storage areas. Small items are easy to miss when furniture and boxes are being removed.

It is also useful to take photos of the apartment before the move. This creates a record of the condition of the property before everything is cleared.

Prepare the Building Access

Apartment moves depend heavily on building access.

Check whether the building requires prior approval for the move, whether a service lift needs to be booked, and where the moving truck can park. Some buildings have specific loading areas or time restrictions.

These arrangements should be confirmed before moving day.

If access is limited to certain hours, the moving crew needs to know the schedule so loading does not start too late.

Protect the Common Areas

The moving route is not limited to your apartment.

Furniture and boxes may pass through corridors, lifts, lobbies and entrances. These areas can become crowded quickly when several large items are being carried at once.

Protective materials can help reduce the chance of marks or scratches, particularly around lift entrances and narrow corners.

It is also worth keeping the route clear. Shoes, small furniture, loose boxes and decorative items should be moved away from the path.

Separate Furniture That Needs Dismantling

Some beds, wardrobes, tables and shelving units are easier to move after being dismantled.

Identify these items before the crew starts loading. Keep screws and small fittings together in labeled bags so they do not disappear during the move.

A simple label such as "bedroom wardrobe screws" is much more useful than putting everything into one unmarked bag.

Check the New Apartment

Before unloading begins, inspect the new apartment.

Check the rooms, doors, flooring and access route. Make sure the movers know where the main furniture should go.

If the new apartment has a different layout from the old one, decide where large furniture belongs before it comes through the door.

This prevents the same sofa or wardrobe from being moved several times.

Keep a Box for the First Night

The first box you open should contain practical items rather than decorative belongings.

Useful contents include:

  • Toilet paper
  • Towels
  • Basic cleaning supplies
  • Chargers
  • Plates and cups
  • Basic kitchen utensils
  • Bed sheets
  • Basic toiletries

Mark this box clearly so it can be found immediately.

Keep the Move Organized

A moving day checklist is useful because it removes small decisions from an already busy day. The goal is not to create more paperwork. It is to know what has been packed, what stays with you, what needs special handling and what must happen before the truck leaves.

Lift bookings, access timing, narrow corridors, as you know apartment moves come with logistics that have nothing to do with the actual packing.
